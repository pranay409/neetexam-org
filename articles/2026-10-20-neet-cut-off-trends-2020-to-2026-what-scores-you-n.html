Understanding NEET Cut-off Trends: The Complete Picture

If you're preparing for NEET, you've probably wondered what score you actually need to get into a good medical college. The truth is that NEET cut-offs have been evolving over the years, and understanding these trends is crucial for setting realistic goals and planning your preparation strategy effectively.

Since 2020, we've seen significant changes in NEET cut-off patterns. The number of test-takers has increased dramatically, competition has intensified, and the qualifying marks have shifted in interesting ways. Let me walk you through what we've observed and what it means for your preparation.

The NEET Cut-off Story: 2020 to 2026

Over the past few years, NEET cut-offs have generally moved upward. In 2020, the general category cut-off was around 138-139 marks out of 720. By 2024, we saw this climb to approximately 165-170 marks. This isn't just random fluctuation—it reflects the increasing difficulty of the exam and the growing number of well-prepared candidates competing for seats.

What These Numbers Actually Mean for You

Looking at this data, several patterns emerge. First, the cut-off has been rising by approximately 5-8 marks each year. This means if you're preparing in 2025 or 2026, you should realistically aim for a score of 170-180 to be comfortable with general category NEET seats.

However, here's what's important to understand: these are all-India cut-offs. Your actual requirement depends on which state and college you're targeting. State-level cut-offs can be significantly lower than all-India figures, and private colleges often have their own merit lists.

Key Insight: While the all-India general cut-off is rising, state quota seats often have lower cut-offs. If your goal is securing any medical seat (not necessarily at a top institute), focusing on state-level opportunities can give you better chances with a score between 150-160.

Factors Driving Cut-off Changes

You need to understand why these cut-offs keep moving. The primary reason is increasing competition. NEET registrations have grown from around 9 lakh in 2020 to over 13 lakh in 2024. More qualified candidates competing for roughly the same number of seats naturally pushes cut-offs higher.

Additionally, exam difficulty fluctuates. When the exam is perceived as easier, cut-offs rise because more students score well. When it's tougher, they might dip slightly. The NMC (National Medical Commission) has also been gradually increasing the total number of seats available, but this increase hasn't kept pace with the growth in applicants.

Strategic Planning Based on Cut-off Trends

Here's what I tell every serious NEET aspirant: don't just chase the minimum cut-off. That's a recipe for disappointment. If the current cut-off is 170, you should aim for at least 190-200 to have comfortable options in college selection.

Why? Because cut-offs represent the bare minimum to qualify. They don't guarantee a good college. With a score of 170, you might get a seat, but it could be in a distant location or a college ranked far below where you could have gotten with a higher score.

Plan your preparation with a target score of 600+ out of 720. This puts you comfortably above anticipated cut-offs and opens doors to better colleges across India.
