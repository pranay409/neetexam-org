Understanding NEET Cut-offs: The Real Picture

When I talk to NEET aspirants, the most common anxiety I see is around cut-off scores. Everyone wants to know: what's the minimum I need to score to get into a good medical college? The truth is, cut-offs aren't fixed numbers handed down from heaven. They change every year based on exam difficulty, number of applicants, and seat availability. But there are clear patterns you can learn from.

Looking at NEET performance data from 2020 to 2026, we can see how cut-offs have evolved. In 2020, the General category cut-off for NEET-UG was around 138 marks out of 720. By 2022, it had risen to 165 marks. By 2024, we saw it stabilize around 180-190 marks. This isn't random. The difficulty level of the paper, the total number of qualified candidates, and the number of available seats all influence this number.

Key Reality Check: Cut-offs vary significantly across states and colleges. Scoring at the national cut-off gets you qualified, but it doesn't guarantee admission to the college of your choice. For premium colleges like AIIMS and top government medical institutions, you need scores in the 600-650+ range.

What These Trends Tell You

The Upward Trajectory is Real

Cut-offs have been rising steadily. From 138 in 2020 to projected 200+ by 2026, the benchmark keeps shifting upward. Why? Because NEET is becoming more predictable and students are preparing better. More focused preparation means more people score higher, which naturally pushes cut-offs up. This is actually good news if you're reading this now, because it means effective preparation works.

The Gap Between Cut-off and Great Scores

Notice the huge gap between the cut-off (around 185) and top scorer range (705-720). This tells you something critical: getting past the cut-off isn't your real goal. Your real goal should be scoring in the 550+ range to get decent college choices, and 650+ for premium institutions. The cut-off is just the floor. Most serious aspirants should aim significantly higher.

Actionable Insight: Stop thinking about "passing" NEET. Think about competing for the best seats. If you aim for 550+, you'll likely clear any state quota. If you aim for 650+, AIIMS and top government colleges become realistic.

How Different Scores Translate to College Options

650+ Score Range

This is the premium tier. You're looking at AIIMS Delhi, Bombay, other top AIIMS institutes, and state government medical colleges' best seats. Your options are genuinely wide.

550-649 Score Range

Excellent state government medical colleges become available. Private college scholarships improve significantly. You have solid options in most states.

400-549 Score Range

You clear the cut-off and get into medical college, but choices are limited. Private colleges and smaller state colleges in your state will be your options. Still, you get into a medical college.

Below 400 Score Range

You'll likely fall below the cut-off. This is where dedicated coaching and strategy adjustments become essential.
