Understanding NEET Cut-off: The Reality Check

Let me be direct with you: understanding NEET cut-off trends isn't just about knowing numbers. It's about strategically positioning yourself to compete effectively. Since 2020, we've seen significant shifts in how cut-offs have evolved, and these patterns tell us something crucial about what you need to score.

The NEET cut-off is the minimum qualifying score set by the National Testing Agency (NTA) that determines your eligibility for medical college counselling. But here's what many aspirants miss: there's the official cut-off and then there's the realistic cut-off you need to aim for if you want a decent college.

Cut-off Trends from 2020 to 2026

2020: The COVID Anomaly

In 2020, the cut-off for General category was 138 out of 720 marks. This sounds surprisingly low, but understand the context: the exam pattern changed, the difficulty level was different, and fewer students appeared. The OBC and SC/ST cut-offs were proportionally lower too.

Key Insight: Low official cut-offs don't mean easier admission. That year, students needed 450+ marks for decent AIIMS or Delhi colleges because competition for top colleges was fiercer.

2021-2022: The Stabilization Phase

By 2021, cut-offs stabilized around 137-150 for General category students. However, what changed dramatically was the number of applicants and their preparation quality. The competition intensified significantly. Students aiming for NIT-tier medical colleges needed scores between 400-450.

2023-2024: Rising Standards

This is where the real shift happened. Cut-offs remained officially similar (around 145-155), but the actual scores needed to secure good colleges jumped to 500-650. Why? Increased competition, better preparation resources, and more serious aspirants appearing for the exam.

Critical Data Point: In 2023-24, General category cut-offs hovered around 147, but students scoring 350-400 rarely got AIIMS or top government medical colleges. The difference between qualifying and getting a good seat became a chasm.

2025-2026: The Current Scenario

Based on current trends and expert analysis, we're expecting cut-offs to remain around 140-150 for General category, but realistic scores for competitive colleges are likely to be 520-650+. Here's why: every year, approximately 10-15 lakh students apply, about 15-20 lakh take the exam, but only around 1-1.5 lakh get quality college seats. The mathematics is brutal.

What Scores Actually Mean for You

Score Ranges and Realistic Outcomes

Below 300: You're not likely to qualify, regardless of category. This requires serious reassessment.
300-400: You'll likely qualify but face state-level or private medical colleges only.
400-500: Good state government colleges become accessible. Not AIIMS yet, but respectable medical education.
500-600: AIIMS and top NLHCs become within reach depending on your category and state preference.
600+: You're in the elite bracket. AIIMS Delhi, top AIIMs, and premier colleges across India are your playground.

The Category Advantage

If you belong to OBC, SC, or ST category, your cut-off is lower, but so is the competition within your category. Over the past six years, we've seen OBC cut-offs around 110-125, SC around 100-115, and ST around 95-110. However, this doesn't mean you should aim lower. Many OBC/SC/ST students score 500+ and secure premium seats because they prepare competitively.
